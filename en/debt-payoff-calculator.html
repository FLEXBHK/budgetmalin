The debt snowball method

Pay the minimum on every debt and put all your extra money on the smallest balance. Once it is paid off, its payment rolls into the next debt: the "snowball" grows. Quick wins come early, which helps enormously to stay on track.

The debt avalanche method

Same principle, but you target the debt with the highest interest rate first (often a credit card). Mathematically, it is the cheapest method.

Which one should I choose?

If the difference in interest is small, choose the snowball: motivation matters more than a few dollars. If you have a high-rate credit card, the avalanche can save a lot.

Frequently asked questions

Should I save or pay off debt first?

Build a small emergency fund first (around one month of expenses) so a surprise bill doesn't push you back into debt, then attack high-interest debt.

What if I can't keep up with payments?

Contact your lenders early to ask about a payment plan. Free, independent debt advice services exist in most countries: look for a non-profit debt charity or credit counselling service near you.
