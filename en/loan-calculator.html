How is the monthly payment calculated?

For a standard amortising loan, the payment stays the same every month. It is calculated with the formula M = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the amount borrowed, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n the number of payments.

In the early years, most of each payment goes to interest. Over time, more and more goes to repaying the principal.

How much can I afford to borrow?

Lenders look at your debt-to-income ratio: the share of your income that goes to debt repayments. Rules vary by country and lender, but many aim to keep housing costs below roughly 28 to 35% of gross income, and total debt payments below roughly 36 to 43%.

Frequently asked questions

How can I reduce the cost of my loan?

Shorten the term, compare several lenders or use a broker, make a bigger down payment, and make extra payments when you can (check for early repayment fees first).

Is a shorter loan always better?

A shorter term means higher monthly payments but much less interest overall. Choose the shortest term whose payment you can comfortably afford while keeping an emergency fund.
