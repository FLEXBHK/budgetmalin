What is zakat al-Fitr?

Zakat al-Fitr (also called sadaqat al-Fitr or fitrana) is an obligatory charity given at the end of Ramadan. It purifies the fasting person from shortcomings during the month and allows those in need to celebrate Eid with dignity.

How much should I give?

Traditionally it is one sa' (about 2.5 to 3 kg) of the local staple food: wheat, rice, dates, barley… Some schools of jurisprudence accept its value in money, others prefer food. The amount in money is set each year by religious bodies in each country.

When should I pay it?

It must be given before the Eid al-Fitr prayer. It is permissible to give it a day or two before Eid, and many mosques collect it from the second half of Ramadan so they have time to distribute it.

Frequently asked questions

Who has to pay it?

Every Muslim who, on the day of Eid, has more than their basic needs. The head of the household pays for themselves and for those they support, including children.

Do I pay for a baby born during Ramadan?

Yes, according to most scholars, for a child born before sunset on the last day of Ramadan. For an unborn child it is recommended but not obligatory.

What is the difference with zakat al-mal?

Zakat al-mal is due on wealth (2.5% above the nisab, once a year). Zakat al-Fitr is a fixed amount per person linked to the end of Ramadan. Calculate your zakat al-mal here.
