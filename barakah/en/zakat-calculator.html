Who has to pay zakat?

Zakat al-mal is due from every adult, sane Muslim whose net wealth reaches the nisab and stays above it for a full lunar year (the hawl). The rate is 2.5% of zakatable wealth.

Gold or silver nisab?

The nisab is the value of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver. Because the silver nisab is much lower, more people become liable: many scholars recommend it for cash as a precaution and in the interest of the poor. Others use gold. Our calculator lets you choose.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay zakat on my home and my car?

No. Personal-use assets (your home, car, furniture, clothes) are not zakatable. Property bought to be resold, however, counts as business stock.

How do I count shares and investment funds?

If you actively trade, count the full market value. For long-term investments, many scholars only count the zakatable portion of the company's assets (often estimated at around 25 to 30% of the value). Opinions differ: ask a scholar you trust.

Who can receive zakat?

The Qur'an (Surah at-Tawbah, 9:60) names eight categories of recipients, including the poor and the needy. You can give directly or through a trusted charity.
